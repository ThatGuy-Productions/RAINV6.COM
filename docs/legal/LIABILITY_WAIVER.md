## 8. Specific Processing Risk Disclaimers

### 8.1 Audio Artefacts

AI and DSP-based audio processing can introduce artefacts including, but not limited to:

- Pre-ringing and post-ringing (linear-phase filter artefacts)
- Temporal smearing (transient softening)
- Inter-sample peaks exceeding true-peak limits
- Codec-specific artefacts after platform re-encoding
- Phase distortion (particularly with extreme stereo width settings)
- Pumping/breathing (overly aggressive compression)
- Frequency masking (excessive EQ in competing bands)

**We make no warranty that RAIN's output will be free from artefacts.**

### 8.2 Loudness Penalties

Streaming platforms apply loudness normalisation and may penalise or reject content that does not meet their loudness standards. RAIN targets platform-specific loudness values, but:

- Platform loudness targets change over time
- Different platforms use different normalisation algorithms
- Some platforms apply additional processing (e.g., Spotify's -14 LUFS normalisation with -1 dBTP limiting)
- RAIN's loudness targeting may not exactly match platform requirements at time of distribution

We are not liable for any loudness penalties, normalisation artefacts, or platform-imposed processing applied after RAIN mastering.

### 8.3 Format Conversion Degradation

Audio distributed through streaming platforms undergoes transcoding (e.g., WAV → AAC for Apple Music, WAV → Ogg Vorbis for Spotify). RAIN does not and cannot control:

- The transcoding quality of downstream platforms
- The codec choice and encoder settings of DSPs
- Audio quality degradation during format conversion
- Metadata preservation during transcoding

### 8.4 Spatial Audio Limitations

RAIN's spatial processing (`src/lib/rain/spatial.ts`) is designed for stereo and binaural applications. It does not produce Dolby Atmos, Sony 360 Reality Audio, or other immersive audio formats. We make no warranty regarding spatial audio compatibility.
